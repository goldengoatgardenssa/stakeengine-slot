## What the pictures do

Hacksaw’s public identity is hand-drawn and a little crude on purpose: thick comic outlines, a short palette, grain instead of glossy 3D, and a silhouette you can name at thumbnail size (a raccoon, a punk with X eyes, a golden Egyptian hand, cartoon mobsters, a dork in a unit). Darkness and one bright accent do more than a rainbow. Paperclip draws every game itself and changes drawing style per title — toon, pixel, neon, stained glass — but each cover is one character or object, large, with a hard silhouette. Both studios look authored. Neither looks like a smooth plastic render.

## HUD and menus

- The grid is the hero. UI chrome is a dark or paper band, not a second illustration fighting the reels.
- Bottom bar, left to right: balance, win, a bet control that opens a menu of amounts, a large circular spin, then autoplay and the feature.
- Bonus entry is a panel of cards, not a tiny text link. Wanted shows three priced paths (80×, 200×, 400×) with the cost on the card and a confirm before the debit. Le Bandit’s site separates a cheap “hunt” (3× for better odds) from bonuses you cannot buy. The card states the cost and what you receive.
- Autoplay asks, then shows a stop. Turbo exists and stays readable.
- Info is a real page: how it plays, a paytable with the pictures, and the RTP and max win on the same surface. Le Bandit’s own page prints volatility, win type, max win 10,000×, and the RTP list.
- Sound is a persistent toggle, not buried.

## Motion

- Scatter landing is a beat: the reel that can complete the feature slows, the frame glows, and the sound rises. Wanted’s duel and Le Bandit’s rainbow are staged so a viewer knows why the screen just got louder.
- Wins are not a static outline. Symbols squash, coins or sparks leave the cell, and the win number rolls instead of popping to the total.
- Big / mega / epic are full-screen moments with a counter, then the reels return. Free-spin intros name the feature and the spin count; outros roll the feature total. Retriggers get their own banner, not a silent counter change.
- Multipliers are a badge that grows on screen (Wanted’s stacked reel multipliers, Le Bandit’s pots). The number is large enough to read across a room.
- Idle motion is small and looped so the grid feels alive before the first spin. Turbo shortens this; it does not delete the result.

## What Copperbell will take, and what it will not

Take: thick ink, a five-color palette, paper grain, one silhouette per symbol, a bottom bar with a feature card, a confirm step, anticipation on the last reels, a roll-up, and intro/outro cards.

Leave: every character, the wordmarks, VS duels, golden squares, rainbows, and any Western, punk, Egyptian, or raccoon costume. The orchard, the copper bell, and the lantern are the whole identity.
